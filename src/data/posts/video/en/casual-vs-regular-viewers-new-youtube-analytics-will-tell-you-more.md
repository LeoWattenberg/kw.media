## New Granular Audience Segments in YouTube Analytics

YouTube is introducing a more detailed way for Creators to understand their viewership patterns. While [YouTube Analytics](/youtube-tips-en/new-in-youtube-analytics-impressions-for-new-and-returning-viewers/) has previously distinguished between new and returning viewers, the platform is now splitting these groups into more granular categories. This update allows Creators to move beyond a simple binary of "new vs. old" and instead analyze the actual depth of viewer engagement over time.

The updated segmentation divides the audience into three primary categories: New Viewers, Casual Viewers, and Regular Viewers.

### Defining Casual vs. Regular Viewers

The distinction between these segments is based on a 12-month rolling window of activity. This provides a clearer picture of viewer loyalty and how often a person returns to a channel.

*   **Casual Viewers:** These are viewers who have engaged with the channel occasionally, specifically for one to five months out of the last twelve months. Because this group encompasses anyone who isn't entirely new but hasn't yet reached long-term consistency, it is expected to be the largest audience segment for most Creators.
*   **Regular Viewers:** This category is reserved for those who have watched the channel for more than six months out of the last twelve. According to insights from Creator Insider, reaching this milestone is significantly more difficult than becoming a casual viewer. Consequently, Creators should expect their "Regular Viewer" count to be considerably lower than their "Casual Viewer" count.

### Why This Segmentation Matters

For a Creator, understanding these segments is crucial for tailoring content and growth strategies. If a channel has a massive number of Casual Viewers but very few Regular Viewers, it may indicate that while the content is discoverable and appealing enough to bring people back occasionally, there may be a gap in "stickiness" or community building that prevents viewers from becoming long-term regulars.

Conversely, a high count of Regular Viewers suggests a strong, loyal core audience that provides a stable foundation for the channel's performance regardless of algorithm fluctuations.

### Rollout Timeline and Availability

The rollout for these new audience segments began this week. However, it is important to note that updates to [YouTube Studio](/en/youtube-tips/) often happen in waves. While some may see these changes immediately, others—including many agency clients—may not have access yet. The full rollout is expected to be completed within the next four to six weeks. Creators are encouraged to monitor their analytics dashboards periodically to see when these new segments become available for their specific channels.

## Enhanced Comment Filtering in YouTube Studio

Alongside the analytics updates, YouTube is improving how Creators manage their community interactions through an upgraded comment filter. This update applies to both the desktop version of YouTube Studio and the YouTube Studio Mobile app.

### Introducing "Most Relevant" Sorting

Previously, Creators primarily relied on sorting comments by "Newest Comment" to keep up with recent conversations. The new update introduces a "Most Relevant" filter. This allows Creators to surface comments that the platform deems most significant, rather than simply those that were posted most recently.

One of the key benefits of this update is the ability to set "Most Relevant" as the default view. However, YouTube is maintaining Creator autonomy regarding these settings; the update will not automatically overwrite existing defaults. If a Creator prefers to see the newest comments first, they can keep that setting active without interference.

### Practical Benefits for High-Volume Channels

While sorting by "Newest" works well for smaller channels or those with moderate engagement, it can become overwhelming for Creators who receive a high volume of unfiltered comments. For these users, scrolling through hundreds of recent posts to find meaningful feedback or urgent questions can be time-consuming.

The "Most Relevant" filter serves as a valuable tool for efficiency, allowing high-volume Creators to prioritize the most impactful interactions first. This ensures that the most important community discussions are addressed without the Creator having to manually sift through every single entry.

By giving Creators more control over how they sort and view their feedback, YouTube is providing a more scalable way to manage community growth as a channel expands.
