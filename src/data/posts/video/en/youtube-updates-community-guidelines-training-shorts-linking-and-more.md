## Navigating the New YouTube Community Guidelines Warning System

For many creators, the most pressing update is the "elephant in the room": the overhaul of the YouTube Community Guidelines warning system. Historically, when a creator violated a YouTube policy, they received a warning that was typically permanent. This could create a lingering cloud over a channel's standing, leaving creators with little recourse once a mistake was made.

Under the new system, YouTube is introducing a path to redemption. Creators can now reverse a warning by completing a specific training module. Once the training is finished, the warning is removed, and the channel returns to good standing. 

However, this "clean slate" comes with a significant caveat. To ensure that the training is effective and not simply a loophole, YouTube has implemented a 90-day probationary period. If a creator violates the same policy again within 90 days of completing the training, the warning becomes permanent and cannot be removed. This means creators must be extra vigilant about the specific rules they have just been trained on to avoid a permanent mark on their account.

Furthermore, the system has become more granular. Instead of a general warning, creators now receive separate warnings for different types of policy violations. For example, if a creator is flagged for a violation in one area and then subsequently flagged for spam or misleading content, these will be treated as two distinct warnings. While this provides more clarity on exactly which rules are being broken, it also means creators could potentially accumulate multiple warnings across different policy categories.

## Expanding Reach with Content Linking in Shorts

Another major update is the full rollout of Content Linking for all creators. This feature addresses a long-standing point of contention, as YouTube had previously limited the use of links in Shorts to prevent spam and security issues. 

The rollout is now complete, allowing creators to better connect their short-form content with their broader library. To manage these links, creators can head to YouTube Studio. The linking options are located in the same area where you would typically find end cards and K2 (cards), making the workflow intuitive for those already familiar with the Studio interface.

The selection screen is straightforward, designed to minimize the time spent in the backend and eliminate downtime. By allowing creators to link directly from a Short to another video, YouTube is effectively bridging the gap between discovery (Shorts) and deep-dive consumption (long-form videos).

## Updates on Clips and Desktop Availability

While many features are rolling out smoothly, some have hit a slight snag. The updates for Clips and the desktop experience have been delayed. Originally expected to arrive last Monday, these features have been pushed back to September 4th. Creators should expect to see these changes go live on Monday evening. 

As Clips become more widely available, creators should consider how to integrate them into their strategy—whether for highlighting the best moments of a long stream or creating shareable snippets for external promotion.

## The Rollout of Vertical Live and YPP Fan Funding

YouTube is also gradually updating the live streaming and monetization experience, though these updates are being released in phases.

### Vertical Live Experience
The Vertical Live Experience is currently in a gradual rollout phase. At present, it is available to approximately 20% of viewers (roughly every 15th viewer). This suggests a cautious approach by YouTube to ensure stability before the feature is pushed to the entire global audience.

### YPP Fan Funding
Simultaneously, a new wave of features for the YouTube Partner Program (YPP) is being deployed, specifically focusing on fan funding. Currently, about 20% of creators have gained access to these new monetization tools. 

It is important to note that regional availability varies. As of now, German creators are not yet included in this initial wave, though they are expected to be part of the subsequent phases. YouTube expects the full rollout of these YPP features to be completed by the end of November. 

Creators should keep an eye on their Community or Forum posts in September, as YouTube is expected to release an official update regarding the timeline and availability of these funding tools. For those waiting on these features, staying tuned to official YouTube communications will be the best way to know when fan funding becomes available in their specific region.
