## New Engagement Tools and Format Shifts for YouTube Shorts

YouTube is continuing to iterate on its short-form video strategy, introducing new ways for creators to spark community interaction and reconsidering the very definition of what constitutes a "Short." From experimental stickers that drive [user-generated content](/en/creator/) to discussions about extending video length, these updates signal a push toward deeper viewer participation.

### Boosting Community Interaction with the "Add Yours" Sticker

One of the most notable experiments currently underway is the introduction of the "[Add Yours" sticker](/youtube-tips-en/ai-summaries-for-comments-and-why-you-dont-see-the-add-yours-sticker/). This feature is designed to act as a direct call-to-action (CTA), inviting viewers to contribute their own perspective or content to a story started by the creator.

When a creator uses this sticker, they are essentially prompting their audience to submit their own Shorts in response to a specific theme or question. For viewers, clicking on the sticker allows them to see other responses and add their own. These viewer-submitted videos are then marked as replies to the original Short.

#### Reach and Visibility
A key point of uncertainty regarding this experiment is how these reply videos are distributed. It remains unclear whether these submissions will only be visible to those who click the "Add Yours" sticker on the original video, or if a viewer-submitted Short can generate its own independent reach within the wider YouTube ecosystem. While it is reasonable to assume that these videos could potentially find their own audience independently, this has not yet been confirmed.

#### Privacy Considerations for Creators
While the "Add Yours" sticker is a powerful tool for generating user-generated content (UGC), creators should be mindful of the privacy implications. Because these responses are public, some viewers may feel uncomfortable responding to a prompt if they do not wish to have their content linked publicly to a specific thread. When encouraging your community to participate, it is worth considering that not every viewer will want to engage in this public manner.

#### The Competitive Landscape
The "Add Yours" functionality is not a novel concept in the social media space; it is a feature heavily utilized by Instagram. [YouTube's adoption of similar tools](/en/youtube-tips/) suggests a desire to match the engagement capabilities of its competitors. However, some may argue that by frequently adding features inspired by other platforms, YouTube risks spreading its resources too thin rather than focusing on perfecting a unique direction for its own ecosystem.

### The Debate Over Longer Shorts

Beyond interactive stickers, there is ongoing discussion regarding the duration of Shorts. In a recent interview on the Creator Insider channel, YouTube's Chief Product Officer, Johanna, discussed the possibility of [longer Shorts](/youtube-tips-en/long-shorts-are-not-displayed-correctly-in-youtube-studio-memberships-updates-and-more/).

This potential change raises a fundamental conceptual question: what defines a "Short"? Currently, there is a distinction between uploading a standard vertical video and uploading a Short to utilize specific Shorts-only features. 

#### The Case for "Verticals"
If YouTube continues to increase the maximum length of Shorts, the term "Shorts" may become a misnomer. A more logical conclusion would be to rebrand these as "Verticals." By shifting the terminology, YouTube could offer a consistent vertical video experience where features are available regardless of whether the video is 15 seconds or several minutes long. This would resolve the current tension between the "Shorts" branding and the desire for longer content, especially since uploading longer vertical videos is already possible on the platform.

### Managing Channel Info and Legal Compliance

Outside of the Shorts ecosystem, YouTube has implemented changes to how contact information is handled in channel info boxes. 

#### The Contact Email Captcha Issue
In the channel settings, creators have the option to provide a contact email address. Traditionally, users could view this address on the channel page after solving a captcha. However, there are reports that while the option to enter the email still exists in the backend, the mechanism for viewers to solve the captcha and display the email is currently non-functional. It remains unclear if this is an intentional design change or a bug resulting from recent UI updates.

#### Importance of the Digital Services Act (DSA)
This technical glitch has practical implications for creators who are subject to provider identification obligations. Under paragraph 5 of the Digital Services Act (DSA), certain creators must ensure they can be reached electronically and quickly.

To remain compliant with these regulations, creators should take the following steps:
1. **Verify your contact settings:** Check if your email is correctly entered in your channel info.
2. **Provide alternatives:** Since the captcha system may be unreliable, ensure you have a clear link to your [legal notice (Impressum)](/en/imprint-service/) where a valid email address is provided.
3. **Direct visibility:** Consider including your contact email directly in your channel description or "About" section to ensure you are reachable as required by law.

Whether these updates to Shorts and channel management are welcomed or viewed with skepticism, they highlight YouTube's ongoing effort to balance community engagement with regulatory requirements and competitive feature parity.
