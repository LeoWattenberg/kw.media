## New Tools for Shorts and Affiliate Marketing

YouTube continues to refine its ecosystem for creators, focusing heavily on streamlining the production process for Shorts and expanding [monetization opportunities](/en/creator/) through shopping integrations.

### Saving Sounds in the Shorts Feed
One of the most practical updates comes to the YouTube Shorts feed. Creators now have the ability to save sounds and music directly from the feed for later use within the Shorts editor. 

Previously, finding a trending sound often required creators to manually search for it or remember the track when they finally sat down to edit. By allowing creators to curate a library of saved audio while browsing, YouTube is reducing the friction between inspiration and execution. This allows you to bookmark audio that fits your brand or current trends and implement them seamlessly during your next production cycle.

### Expanding Affiliate Opportunities with Chrome Extension
For creators based in the United States, YouTube is introducing a new Chrome Extension designed to simplify the process of [affiliate marketing](/en/ads/). 

YouTube Shopping in the US operates similarly to the Amazon Affiliate Program; it allows creators to link specific products below their videos using affiliate links, earning a commission on resulting sales. The new Chrome Extension acts as a bridge between browsing and content creation. When you encounter a product online that would be a good fit for your audience and the affiliate program, the extension makes it easier to identify and integrate those products into your shopping workflow.

However, there is a significant caveat: this extension and the broader affiliate program are currently limited to the USA. For creators in other regions, this means continuing to rely on competitor tools or alternative affiliate methods until YouTube expands these features globally.

## Enhancing Live Stream Interaction and AI Integration

Live streaming remains a cornerstone of community building on YouTube, and several updates are aimed at making these broadcasts more interactive and accessible.

### Super Chat Replies
Interaction within live streams is becoming more conversational with the full rollout of Super Chat replies. As of August 1st, this feature is 100% active.

In the past, a [Super Chat](/youtube-tips-en/coming-soon-to-youtube-custom-thumbs-for-shorts-and-superchat-goals/) was primarily a way for a viewer to get a message highlighted and provide financial support. Now, creators and other viewers can click on a Super Chat to view existing replies or write their own. This transforms the Super Chat from a static shout-out into a threaded conversation, potentially increasing the value of the interaction for the paying viewer and fostering deeper engagement within the chat.

### AI-Powered Live Stream Summaries
YouTube is also implementing AI to generate summaries of live streams, helping viewers catch up on long broadcasts without watching the entire recording. However, this feature comes with specific technical requirements and regional restrictions.

For an AI summary to be generated, the stream must meet a specific activity threshold: there must be more than 30 non-repeating English messages sent within a two-minute window. This high bar—essentially one unique message every two seconds—ensures that the AI has enough diverse data to create a meaningful summary and prevents spam from triggering an inaccurate recap.

It is important to note that due to current AI restrictions, these summaries are not available in the EU. Creators and viewers in the UK and other English-speaking regions may already see this feature, though its availability varies by channel.

## Improving the Live Stream Ad Experience

One of the long-standing pain points for live stream viewers is the "asynchronous experience" caused by traditional mid-roll ads. When a standard ad plays, it completely interrupts the live feed, often causing the viewer to miss critical moments of the broadcast.

### Picture-in-Picture (PiP) Mid-Rolls
Starting August 5th, YouTube is addressing this by introducing picture-in-picture ads for live streams. Instead of a full-screen takeover, the stream screen will be compressed, and the ad will run alongside it, framed by black bars at the top and bottom. Once the ad concludes, the screen expands back to its original size.

This change ensures that while the ad is playing, the viewer remains connected to the live event visually, reducing the frustration of missing content. To further improve this experience, there has been a feature request for [automatic subtitles](/en/video-dubbing/) to be enabled by default during these mid-rolls—a move that would be particularly beneficial for talk shows or podcasts where dialogue is the primary focus.

## Discovery and Engagement: YouTube Hype and Stickers

Beyond technical updates, YouTube is experimenting with new ways to help smaller creators gain visibility and engage their communities.

### Understanding "YouTube Hype"
Currently being tested in Turkey, Taiwan, and Brazil, [YouTube Hype](/youtube-tips-en/youtube-updates-new-shorts-captions-and-hype-feature-expansion/) is a discovery mechanism designed to boost smaller channels. While it was originally considered as a paid feature to push videos into the trending tab, it has evolved into a viewer-driven interaction tool.

To be eligible for "Hype," a channel must meet the following criteria:
*   Have fewer than 500,000 subscribers.
*   Be a member of the YouTube Partner Program.
*   Have no active community guideline violations.
*   The video must have been uploaded within the last seven days.

When viewers "hype" a qualifying video, that content is displayed in a dedicated section on the Explore page. This creates a pathway for emerging creators to reach new audiences based on viewer enthusiasm rather than just algorithmic suggestions or paid promotion.

### "Add Yours" Stickers
Finally, YouTube is rolling out "Add Yours" stickers, a feature designed to spark trends and community participation. These stickers are currently in full rollout and are expected to be available to all creators by the end of August or the beginning of September. 

These interactive elements encourage viewers to contribute their own content to a shared theme, mirroring successful engagement patterns seen on other social platforms and providing a low-friction way for you to start a community challenge or conversation.
