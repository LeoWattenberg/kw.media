## New Text-to-Speech Options for YouTube Shorts on Android

YouTube is currently experimenting with a new text-to-speech (TTS) feature integrated directly into the [Shorts Editor](/youtube-tips-en/creating-shorts-on-youtube-youtube-tutorial-shorts-2/) within the Android app. This update allows creators to convert their on-screen text into spoken audio, providing an automated way to add voiceovers to short-form content without needing to record a physical vocal track.

For now, this functionality is exclusive to Android users. Those using iOS devices will have to wait for a potential rollout to their platform. According to current information, the feature is being deployed gradually over the next few weeks. If you are an Android user and do not see the option in your editor yet, it is recommended to check back in a week or two as the update reaches more accounts.

### How the Feature Works

The integration is designed to be seamless within the existing [Shorts Editor](/youtube-tips-en/youtube-updates-new-shorts-captions-and-hype-feature-expansion/). Once a creator inserts text onto their video, they can choose to have that text read aloud. To provide some variety in tone and style, YouTube is offering four different voices for creators to choose from. This allows for a degree of customization depending on the mood or target audience of the specific Short.

## Practical Applications for Creators

The addition of native text-to-speech opens up several possibilities for creators who may face barriers when recording traditional voiceovers.

### Overcoming Recording Hurdles
One of the most immediate benefits is for creators who are uncomfortable with the sound of their own voice. Many people find it difficult to record narration due to a lack of confidence in their vocal delivery; TTS provides a professional-sounding alternative that allows them to present their ideas without the psychological barrier of being on mic.

Additionally, TTS serves as a technical workaround. In many filming environments, background noise—such as wind, traffic, or crowd chatter—can make it impossible to record clean audio on location. By using text-to-speech, creators can still provide necessary narration and context for their visuals without the need for an external studio or expensive noise-canceling equipment.

### Testing Educational Formats
The utility of this feature extends to structured content. For example, creating a "glossary" style series—such as a "YouTube ABC"—can be enhanced by TTS. Using automated voices for definitions and short explanations can help maintain a consistent pace and tone across a large volume of short videos, making the information easier for viewers to digest.

## The Importance of Adding Value: A Warning on Monetization

While text-to-speech is a powerful tool, it comes with a significant caveat regarding channel health and monetization. YouTube has indicated that the excessive or mindless use of TTS can lead to negative consequences, including potential exclusion from the [YouTube Partnership Program (YPP)](/en/creator/).

The core issue lies in "added value." YouTube's systems and reviewers want to ensure that a Creator has put genuine effort into their production. There is a risk that creators might use TTS to churn out low-effort content—essentially creating haphazard collections of clips with half-hearted text overlays. 

To avoid issues with the Partnership Program, creators should ensure that:
*   The TTS is used to enhance the storytelling or provide necessary information.
*   The video is not merely an automated assembly of existing content.
*   There is clear evidence of creative work and intentional editing behind the final product.

In short, while automation can assist in the production process, it should not replace the creative input of the Creator.

## Additional Tooling: An Overview of ViewSats

Beyond the internal updates to Shorts, there are external tools currently circulating in the creator community that can help with channel analysis. One such tool is ViewSats, a browser extension developed by MrBeast's team.

ViewSats functions as a hybrid between VIQ and Social Blade, but with the advantage of running directly on YouTube as an extension rather than requiring a separate website visit. Its primary utility is allowing users to see if a specific video is performing better than other videos on the same channel in real-time. 

While it may not be essential for every creator—particularly those who find paid analytics options unnecessary—it can be quite handy for those managing or analyzing large channels with significant viewer bases, where comparing relative performance values provides more meaningful data.
