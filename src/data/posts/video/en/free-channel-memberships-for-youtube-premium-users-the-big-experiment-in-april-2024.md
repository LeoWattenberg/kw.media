## New Updates for YouTube Live, Shorts, and Gaming Integration

YouTube is currently implementing a series of updates across its mobile and desktop platforms, with a heavy emphasis on enhancing user engagement, refining [monetization tools](/en/creator/), and deepening the integration between video content and gaming. From new interaction features in live streams to experimental membership perks for Premium users, these changes signal a shift toward more interactive, mobile-first experiences.

## Enhancing Mobile Live Stream Engagement

YouTube is prioritizing the mobile experience for its [live streaming features](/en/live/), specifically focusing on vertical live streams. One of the most anticipated additions is a new tagging system for co-chatters. 

### Improved Tagging in Vertical Streams
The team is developing a feature that allows users to tag other participants in the chat with just a few keystrokes. Once implemented, these tags will be highlighted in a different color for the recipient, mirroring the functionality already available on the desktop version. This visual distinction ensures that tagged users are immediately aware of the mention, fostering better real-time communication between creators and their community.

While this feature is being rolled out for vertical streams first, YouTube intends to bring these improvements to horizontal live chats as well. However, a specific release date for the horizontal rollout has not yet been established; the focus remains on ensuring the vertical version is stable before expanding.

## Shorts: Desktop Fixes and Mobile-First Thumbnails

For creators utilizing YouTube Shorts, there are several updates regarding how content is displayed and managed across different devices.

### View Count Bug Fix
A persistent bug affecting the desktop feed—where views for Shorts were not being displayed correctly—has been resolved. This ensures that creators and viewers have an accurate representation of a Short's performance when browsing via a computer.

### The Mobile-Only Thumbnail Workflow
Despite requests from the community, it appears that [Shorts thumbnails](/youtube-tips-en/membership-bonus-program-thumbnail-updates-and-ai-dubbing-on-the-horizon/) will not be returning to the desktop version in the near future. YouTube is doubling down on a smartphone-centric approach for Shorts management. 

To customize thumbnails for Shorts, creators must use a mobile device (either Android or iOS). To assist with this, YouTube is introducing a built-in text editor for Shorts thumbnails on mobile. This tool allows creators to overlay text directly onto the frame, providing a way to create more clickable and descriptive covers without needing external editing software. The rollout for this text editor is scheduled between late February and late March 2024.

## Membership Gifting and Premium Experiments

YouTube is testing several ways to increase membership adoption through gifting and bundled perks, though some limitations remain regarding account types and timelines.

### Member Gifting Constraints
The "Member Gifting with 2x Free Memberships" feature currently has strict eligibility requirements. It is only available for main accounts within the YouTube Partner Program (YPP); brand accounts are not currently supported. While this feature was prioritized for the current quarter, there is no activity expected in March, leaving its status for the next quarter uncertain.

Furthermore, "Targeted Member Gifting" has been confirmed as unavailable for Q2 2024.

### Free Memberships for Premium Users
A new experiment is set to launch in April that grants YouTube Premium users a free channel membership. While this concept has appeared previously, it is now moving forward again. 

There is a significant caveat, however: rumors suggest that this may be a one-time perk granted upon upgrading to Premium, rather than a recurring monthly benefit. For creators and users who prefer a monthly rotation of memberships to support different channels, providing feedback during this experimental phase will be critical to signaling the demand for a recurring model over a one-time bonus.

## Super Chat Evolution: Likes and Threads

Transaction revenue through [Super Chats](/youtube-tips-en/youtube-creator-news-live-reminders-paid-hype-and-super-chat-goals/) is receiving a significant upgrade to make paid messages more interactive and organized.

### Super Chat Likes
Starting around March 20th, YouTube will introduce "likes" for Super Chats across both mobile and desktop platforms. This allows the community to acknowledge and elevate high-quality or particularly supportive messages, adding another layer of social validation to the monetization process.

### Replies and Threads
Following the likes update, a more substantial change is expected at the end of April: the introduction of Super Chat replies and threads. While the exact UI remains unknown, this feature will likely allow creators and viewers to respond directly to specific Super Chats, creating organized conversation threads. 

This could be particularly transformative for educational or technical channels—such as those focusing on DIY projects, motorcycle maintenance, or tutorials—where a paid question can trigger a detailed, threaded discussion that provides value to the rest of the audience.

## The Integration of Gaming and "Playables"

YouTube is making an aggressive push into the gaming space, leveraging former Google infrastructure to create a more seamless bridge between watching a game and playing it.

### The Return of YouTube Playables
In approximately 1.5 months, YouTube Playables will return, initially launching in the US and select English-speaking countries. This feature allows YouTube Premium subscribers to play small, integrated games for free directly within the platform.

This move is supported by a shift in talent; several developers from Google's defunct Stadia platform have moved to the YouTube team. They are specifically working on expanding "Crowdplay," a feature that originally allowed Stadia users to join another user's stream. The long-term goal is to integrate a button directly into streams by the end of 2024, allowing viewers to purchase and download games instantly.

### Third-Party Platform Connectivity
To facilitate this ecosystem, YouTube is enabling connections with third-party gaming platforms such as Epic Games and Battlenet. While some details remain speculative, the technical interface between these accounts already exists.

The vision for this integration includes:
*   **Direct Purchases:** The ability to click a button in a stream, buy a game via Battlenet or Epic Games, and immediately join the streamer's session.
*   **Affiliate Revenue:** The potential introduction of affiliate keys. This mirrors the "Creator Code" system used by Epic Games, where creators receive a revenue share when their audience purchases games using their specific code.

For gaming creators, this represents a significant opportunity to diversify income streams beyond traditional ad revenue and memberships by leveraging direct game affiliations.
