A small creator asked why YouTube keeps supporting the channels that already make money, and from February 2027 makes it twice as hard for everyone else to get into the [Partner Program](/youtube-tips-en/youtube-monetization-requirements-doubled-creator-news/). I think that's a fair question, so I did some math: what does a channel cost YouTube before it pays you a single cent?

For a long-form channel right at today's threshold it's about **$6 a year**. Google doesn't publish its costs, so every Google number here is my estimate. You can put in your own channel in our [YouTube cost calculator](/en/tools/youtube-channel-cost/).

## What a small channel costs

A video costs money when it's stored, when it's converted into all the quality levels and when it's delivered to whoever watches. I priced that at four different levels.

| Channel at today's threshold | Google's scale (estimate) | Own servers | Amazon list price | Cloudflare Stream |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Long-form, 4,000 watch hours a year | $6 | $43 | $210 | $280 |
| Shorts, 10 million views in 90 days | $15 | $47 | $1,300 | $4,200 |
| Live, three streams a week of 3 hours | $140 | $360 | $2,100 | $1,900 |

**Shorts** sound like the easy way in. But every view is a video that has to be delivered, plus the Shorts people swipe past and the next ones loading in the background. With 25 seconds per view that's around 69,000 hours, against 4,000 for long-form.

**Live streams** are the most expensive format. Every hour you're live, YouTube converts the stream into all quality levels in nearly real time, even if nobody's watching, and keeps the archive of every stream under 12 hours. With only three viewers it costs about the same. Twitch deletes the same streams after a week or two.

## YouTube earns on you as well

YouTube's Terms of Service have a section called "Right to Monetize". In the US since 2020 and everywhere else since 2021, YouTube can run ads on your videos before you're in the Partner Program and keep all of it. Officially only a limited set of brand-safe videos. How many? They don't say.

With Shorts it's clearer. The ads in the feed play between videos, no matter whose Short comes next. The money is pooled, and the part that falls on non-monetized creators pays for music licenses or stays with YouTube.

My own numbers from Defender833: about 580,000 Shorts views in 12 months got me around 65 euros. Creators get 45% of their share of the pool, so my full share was about 25 cents per 1,000 views. Take a creator who isn't monetized yet and does [10 million Shorts views to qualify](/youtube-tips-en/how-to-get-into-the-partner-program-with-youtube-shorts/). At my rate that's about 2,500 euros that YouTube keeps or spends on music, against roughly 15 dollars of hosting. That's one German gaming channel, so your rate may be different.

## So hosting isn't the reason

Doubling the threshold means YouTube hosts you longer before paying you. The extra 4,000 hours cost under a dollar, the extra 10 million Shorts views about 12. YouTube's official reason is keeping pace with how much the platform has grown. Maybe it's the work of reviewing all those applications, maybe it's the fraud. I don't know, and I won't pretend I do.

## Why nobody builds a second YouTube

Storage and delivery for all of YouTube would be about $15.6 billion a year at Amazon list prices. That's 39% of YouTube's entire ad revenue in 2025. Google pays, at my estimated prices, around $650 million or 1.6%.

Per hour it's even clearer. YouTube makes about 7 cents in ads per hour watched. Delivering that hour through Amazon's CDN costs about 7.7 cents at the first price tier. Smaller CDNs such as Bunny list less than one cent. But you still start behind on both ends: paying list price and without Google's ad network.

[Twitch](/youtube-tips-en/thats-why-you-earn-more-on-youtube-than-on-twitch-revenue-comparison/) is live only, the most expensive format there is. For every hour streamed there, about 21 hours get watched. On YouTube Gaming it's 77, on Kick 81 (Stream Hatchet estimates, the division is mine).

## If you're close to the threshold

Apply before February 1st, 2027. As far as I know, applications that are in before the deadline get reviewed under the old rules.
