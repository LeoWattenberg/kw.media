## YouTube Streamlines Features: The End of the Subscribe Watermark and New Live Tools

YouTube is currently undergoing a series of updates aimed at cleaning up legacy features and refining the user interface for both creators and viewers. From the removal of long-standing branding tools to new monetization goals for vertical live streams, these changes reflect a shift toward mobile optimization and more professional control over live broadcasts.

## The Deprecation of the Subscribe Watermark

In a move that marks the end of an era for channel branding, YouTube is officially deprecating the subscribe button watermark. For years, creators could upload a small, square icon—often a "Subscribe" graphic or a channel logo—that sat in the bottom right corner of their videos. When hovered over on a desktop, this watermark allowed viewers to subscribe without leaving the video player.

### Why is it going away?
YouTube's decision is driven primarily by the shift in viewer behavior toward mobile devices. On mobile platforms, where the vast majority of consumption now happens, the watermark has become largely obsolete. The platform noted that the icon is frequently obscured by other user interface (UI) elements or is simply too small to be an effective call to action. Data indicates that very few viewers were actually clicking on these watermarks, rendering the feature ineffective for [growing a subscriber base](/en/creator/).

### Effective alternatives for growth
With the removal of this legacy tool, creators should lean into more active and visible methods of audience acquisition. If you are looking to replace the functionality of the watermark, consider these proven strategies:
*   **Clear Verbal Calls to Action (CTAs):** Directly asking viewers to subscribe within the video content remains one of the most effective ways to convert a viewer into a subscriber.
*   **End Screens:** Utilizing the final 20 seconds of a video to place a clickable subscribe element is far more visible than a small corner icon.
*   **Pinned Comments:** Using the comment section to remind viewers why they should follow your channel provides a high-visibility touchpoint right below the video player.

From a development perspective, removing this feature allows YouTube to redirect resources away from maintaining an underused legacy tool and toward more impactful platform improvements.

## New Monetization Tools for Vertical Live Streams

While some features are being removed, others are being added—though availability remains limited by region. YouTube is introducing "Gift Goals" specifically for [vertical live](/youtube-tips-en/youtubes-ui-update-confuses-users-and-creators/) streaming.

### Understanding Gift Goals
Gift Goals allow creators to set a specific target for the number of fan funding gifts or "YouTube Jewels" they wish to receive during a broadcast. This creates a visual objective for the community to rally around, potentially increasing engagement and revenue during live events. 

It is important to make a clear distinction here: **Gift Goals are not related to gifted memberships.** These goals apply exclusively to the small, animated gifts that viewers can purchase and send during vertical streams. To keep these interactions fresh, YouTube has also announced a new collection of gift designs arriving this fall, featuring pumpkin-themed animations.

### Regional Availability Caveat
Creators outside of the United States should note that this feature is currently only available in the US. There is currently no confirmed timeline for an international rollout. For most global creators, these updates serve as a preview of potential future tools rather than immediate options for their toolkit.

## YouTube Studio Housekeeping and Moderation Updates

Beyond monetization and branding, YouTube is implementing several quality-of-life improvements within YouTube Studio to make channel management more intuitive.

### Streamlining User Management
YouTube is decluttering the channel moderation settings to reduce visual noise and improve navigation. The primary change involves the "User Management" section; rather than requiring creators to scroll through a long list of settings, User Management now has its own dedicated tab. While this is purely a UI tweak with no changes to actual functionality, it simplifies the workflow for creators who frequently manage their moderators and blocked users.

### Opting Out of Live Leaderboards
In addition to the Studio cleanup, creators now have more granular control over how their [vertical live streams](/youtube-tips-en/youtube-livestream-updates-hype-expansion-and-ai-inspiration-tools-for-eu-creators/) appear to the public. Specifically, YouTube has added an option to turn off leaderboards for a channel.

This feature was developed in response to feedback from corporate entities and news organizations. While leaderboards can be exciting for gaming or entertainment creators, they are often seen as inappropriate or unprofessional for news stations and corporate broadcasts. This opt-out ensures that professional streams can maintain a formal tone while still allowing live chat interaction.
