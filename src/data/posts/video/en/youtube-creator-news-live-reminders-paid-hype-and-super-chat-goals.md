## Enhancing Live Stream Discovery and Monetization: New YouTube Updates

YouTube is continuing to refine the intersection between short-form discovery and long-form live engagement. Recent updates focus on making it easier for viewers to find [scheduled streams](/en/live/) through Shorts, introducing new [monetization avenues](/en/creator/) via "Hype," and improving the mobile management experience for Creators. 

From technical bug fixes in live redirects to experimental features currently testing in specific regions, these changes signal a push toward more integrated community engagement tools.

## Bridging the Gap: Live Stream Reminders in Shorts

One of the most significant updates for streamers is the introduction of official reminders for upcoming livestreams within the Shorts feed. While this was previously discussed as a possibility, YouTube has confirmed that this is now an active experiment rolling out to all Creators.

### How Live Stream Reminders Work
The system is designed to automate the promotion of scheduled events. When a Creator schedules a live stream, a notification prompt will automatically appear across all of that Creator's Shorts, provided the stream is set to begin within the next 24 hours. 

This feature effectively turns every Short into a [promotional tool](/en/ads/) for upcoming live content, reducing the need for Creators to manually create separate announcement videos or community posts to drive traffic to a scheduled event.

### Rollout Timeline
The rollout is following a specific experimental phase:
1. **Testing Phase:** The experiment will run for approximately two weeks.
2. **Official Release:** Following the testing period, the YouTube team is expected to release an official post announcing the wider availability of the feature.

## Managing the Shorts Experience

As YouTube integrates more live features into the Shorts feed, some users may find the volume of short-form content overwhelming. To address this, YouTube has implemented a way for viewers to signal their preference for fewer Shorts.

### Reducing Shorts Visibility
Users can click on the 2x2 box located at the top of the Shorts section within the standard YouTube app to indicate they want to see fewer Shorts. According to YouTube, this action will temporarily reduce the number of Shorts shown in the user's feed.

From a Creator's perspective, there is a concern that this could negatively impact reach and view counts. However, YouTube suggests that the impact on overall numbers should be relatively minimal, as users who opt for this setting are likely individuals who would have watched fewer Shorts regardless of the setting.

### The Temporary Nature of the Filter
A point of contention remains regarding the duration of this filter. Currently, the "see fewer Shorts" preference is only temporary—typically lasting 30 to 60 days—after which the user will begin seeing Shorts in their feed again. It remains unclear why YouTube has opted for a temporary reset rather than allowing users to disable the feature permanently.

## Technical Stability and Live Redirects

Live redirects are essential for streamers who move their audience from one broadcast to another. While there were reports of redirect issues in recent weeks, the YouTube team has identified and fixed the bug.

### Ensuring a Smooth Transition
Despite the fix, technical variances can still occur depending on user settings. To ensure that viewers are redirected properly without interruption, it is highly recommended that streamers encourage their audience to enable the **autoplay** feature. While some reports indicate that redirects may work without autoplay, enabling it provides the safest and most consistent experience for the viewer.

### Addressing the "Shorts Overlay" Glitch
A rare but disruptive bug has been noted for smartphone users. In certain instances, viewers who have been watching Shorts before joining a live stream may find themselves stuck in a "Shorts overlay" even while viewing a horizontal stream. This creates a strange visual conflict where the user is technically in the stream but the interface remains in the vertical Shorts format. Currently, the only reliable workaround for this issue is to completely close and reopen the YouTube app.

## New Monetization Frontiers: Paid Hypes

The "Hype" feature was introduced as a way for viewers to support Creators using a limited number of free Hypes (three per week). However, YouTube is now testing an expansion of this system through **Paid Hypes**.

### Expanding Support Capabilities
Paid Hypes allow viewers to pay for the ability to distribute more than the three free Hypes provided weekly. This allows highly engaged fans to provide additional visibility and support to their favorite Creators beyond the standard limits.

### Revenue Share and Availability
A critical detail for Creators is the financial structure of this feature. YouTube has confirmed a **70/30 revenue share**, meaning 70% of the transaction revenue from Paid Hypes goes directly to the Creator. By treating these as transaction revenues rather than platform fees, YouTube is positioning the feature as a direct support mechanism for the community.

Currently, Paid Hypes are being tested in **Brazil and Turkey**. Creators in other regions should monitor updates to see when this monetization tool expands globally.

## The Status of Super Chat Goals

Super Chat Goals—a feature allowing streamers to set specific financial targets during a broadcast—have faced several delays (or "pushbacks"). However, progress is currently being made through an experimental phase.

### Current Testing Phase
The feature is presently being tested with a segment of 20% to 30% of the total user base. This phased rollout allows YouTube to gather data on how goals affect viewer behavior and donation patterns before a full release.

### Expected Timeline
While schedules on platform updates can be fluid, the current roadmap suggests:
* **End of Month:** An update from the YouTube team regarding the test results.
* **End of Year:** The expected completion of the full launch.

## Enhancements to YouTube Studio Mobile

For Creators who manage their channels on the go, YouTube Studio Mobile has received a quality-of-life update: the addition of a horizontal (landscape) mode.

While deep analytical dives are typically reserved for desktop environments, the landscape mode significantly improves the visibility of graphics and charts. This allows Creators to get a clearer overview of their performance metrics and visual data without needing to switch to a computer.

## Shaping Future Features: Community Initiative

Beyond technical updates, there is an ongoing effort to bridge the gap between the Creator community and YouTube's management. A current initiative is underway to collect feature requests and pain points for a direct presentation to YouTube management.

### The Process for Feature Requests
The goal is to create a community-driven slide for a management presentation. The process involves:
1. **Idea Collection:** Gathering suggestions from Creators across various niches, including the YouTube Gaming community.
2. **Community Voting:** Creating a comprehensive list of desired features and allowing the community to vote on them.
3. **Submission:** Submitting the top 10 most-requested features directly to YouTube management.

This initiative provides a structured way for Creators to influence the platform's roadmap by highlighting the tools and features that would provide the most value to the widest range of users.
