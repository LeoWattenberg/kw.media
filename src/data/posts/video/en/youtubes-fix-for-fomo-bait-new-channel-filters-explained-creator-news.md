## Improving Discovery and Member Experience: YouTube's Latest Quality-of-Life Updates

YouTube has announced two significant quality-of-life updates designed to refine how viewers discover new content and how paying members navigate exclusive channel offerings. While these updates may seem small in isolation, they address long-standing friction points for both creators and their audiences—specifically regarding [targeted discovery](/en/youtube-tips/) and the management of membership-exclusive content.

Before diving into the technical details, it is worth noting that creator feedback remains a driving force behind these changes. For those who want to share their own challenges and insights, the [Creator Struggle Survey](https://forms.gle/SxGtJhEVBphp4oYN8) is currently open for participation to help gather data on the actual hurdles creators face daily.

## Expanding the Reach of the Hype Feature

The [Hype feature](/youtube-tips-en/youtube-updates-new-shorts-captions-and-hype-feature-expansion/), designed to give smaller creators a boost in visibility, is receiving a set of functional expansions that were first teased last fall. These updates aim to move Hype from a simple interaction to a more robust discovery mechanism.

### Category Leaderboards for Targeted Discovery
One of the most significant additions is the introduction of Category Leaderboards. Rather than relying on general trends, viewers can now browse leaderboards tailored to specific interests, such as gaming or beauty. 

For creators, this means that their content is no longer just competing in a global pool but is being categorized into interest-based buckets. This should lead to more targeted discovery, connecting creators with audiences who are already predisposed to enjoy their specific niche.

### Public Hype Sharing and Community Integration
To further integrate the feature into the social fabric of a channel, YouTube is introducing Public Hype Sharing. When a viewer "hypes" a video, they now have the option to share that action as a public post within the creator's community tab. 

This transforms a private interaction into a public signal of quality. By making these hypes visible in the community tab, fan communities become more accessible and active, allowing other viewers to see what the community is currently excited about and encouraging further engagement.

### Current Limitations and Caveats
Despite these additions, there are important restrictions that creators should keep in mind:
*   **Market Availability:** The Hype feature is not yet available in all global markets.
*   **Platform Restriction:** Currently, Hype is a mobile-only experience. There has been no official word regarding a rollout for desktop users.

The lack of desktop support remains a significant bottleneck, as it limits the potential reach and utility of the feature for a large segment of the viewing audience.

## Solving the 'FOMO-Bait' Problem with Membership Filters

For channels utilizing [YouTube Memberships](/en/creator/), navigating the channel page has historically been a rigid experience. Until now, viewers could only sort content by "Latest," "Popular," or "Oldest." This lack of granularity created a problem for creators who produce a high volume of exclusive content for their paying supporters.

### New Filter Chips: "Members Only" and "Public"
YouTube is introducing two new filter chips to the content tabs on channel pages: **"Members Only"** and **"Public."** 

This update allows members to instantly isolate the exclusive content they are paying for without having to sift through public uploads. Conversely, it allows non-members to view only the public library of a channel with a single click.

### Addressing the "FOMO-Bait" Controversy
This update appears to be a direct response to community feedback and criticisms raised by prominent creators, such as Linus Tech Tips. The core of the issue was the perception that some channels were using member-only content as a tactic—often referred to as "FOMO-bait"—to clutter the feeds of non-members. By flooding the feed with locked videos, creators could theoretically entice viewers to join memberships out of a fear of missing out on essential information.

The introduction of these filter chips provides an elegant solution by giving the viewer control over what they see on the channel page. It separates the "exclusive" experience from the "public" experience, reducing friction for non-paying viewers while improving the user experience for members.

### A Remaining Gap in Recommendations
While the filter chips solve the problem of navigation on the channel page itself, there is a significant caveat: these filters likely will not affect the YouTube recommendation algorithm. This means that member-only content may still appear in the home feeds or "Up Next" suggestions for non-members, potentially continuing the frustration of clicking a video only to find it is locked behind a paywall.

Whether this update fully resolves the tension between membership promotion and viewer experience remains to be seen, but it is a positive step toward better channel management.
