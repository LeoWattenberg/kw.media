## Streamlining Shorts Thumbnails and AI-Driven Discovery

For a long time, creators uploading YouTube Shorts faced a fragmented and tedious workflow when it came to selecting thumbnails. To get a specific frame or a custom image as a thumbnail, many had to edit on a desktop, upload the file to a service like Google Drive, download it onto a smartphone, open the YouTube mobile app, and only then perform the upload to select the desired frame.

This friction is now gone. Creators can finally select their Shorts thumbnails directly within their browser via the desktop studio. When uploading a Short through the desktop interface, you now have two primary options: choosing from three auto-generated frames provided by YouTube or uploading a [custom thumbnail](/en/tools/youtube-thumbnail-preview/), mirroring the workflow used for long-form video content.

### Using "Our Studio" as an Algorithm Diagnostic Tool

Beyond simple selection, the introduction of the "Get Suggestions" button within Our Studio—located directly below the thumbnail selection area—offers a more strategic opportunity for creators. As this feature rolls out to more users, there has been a noticeable increase in thumbnails with a distinct AI-generated aesthetic. While some creators use these suggestions as final assets, others are using them as conceptual blueprints based on the video's transcript.

However, the real value of "Get Suggestions" may not be in the visual output—which can sometimes be mediocre—but in its utility as a diagnostic tool for your channel's discoverability. 

The logic is simple: if Our Studio generates a concept that accurately represents the core essence of your video, it indicates that YouTube has correctly indexed your semantic data. In other words, the AI understands exactly what your video is about, which significantly increases the likelihood that the recommendation algorithm will serve your content to the right audience.

Conversely, if the AI produces irrelevant or inaccurate suggestions, it serves as a warning sign. If the AI cannot comprehend the topic of your video, it is highly probable that YouTube's broader recommendation system will struggle as well. In these instances, creators should treat Our Studio as a "personal algorithm coach." If the suggestions are off the mark, it may be time to revisit and rewrite your script or refine your metadata to ensure the AI can properly categorize the content.

## Twitch’s Shift Toward Vertical Discovery

While YouTube is refining its backend tools for creators, Twitch is attempting to overhaul its discovery system by leaning heavily into a mobile-first, vertical experience. The platform has introduced an endless free-scrolling feed on its mobile app, effectively adopting the TikTok model of content consumption. 

Upon opening the app, users are now greeted with a vertical feed consisting of live streams and clips. For Twitch streamers, this shift changes the importance of clip management. To gain visibility in this new ecosystem, it is no longer enough to simply stream; creators must implement a strategic approach to their VODs (Video on Demand). Exporting high-retention vertical clips from these VODs is now essential for capturing the attention of users scrolling through the mobile feed and driving them back to the live channel.

### The Discoverability Hurdle: Pre-roll Ads

Despite the new scroll feed, a significant barrier remains for new viewers: pre-roll ads. In the context of discovery, pre-roll ads act as "bouncers," often turning potential viewers away before they have the chance to see the actual content of the stream. 

This creates a difficult tension for creators and the platform. Live streaming is an expensive endeavor; broadcasting to thousands of people requires significant server resources that must be paid for, regardless of whether the streamer has a large audience or is broadcasting to an empty room.

To mitigate the "bouncer" effect of pre-rolls while still generating necessary revenue, some creators have adopted a specific workaround: manually running three minutes of mid-roll ads per hour. By shifting the ad load to the middle of the broadcast, streamers can keep the "door open" for new viewers arriving from the mobile feed without immediately hitting them with an advertisement, while simultaneously training their existing audience to take short breaks.

### New Parental Controls and Content Reach

Finally, Twitch has introduced a set of parental controls specifically for teen accounts aged 13 to 17. These tools allow parents to manage several aspects of their child's experience, including:
*   Limiting overall screen time.
*   Blocking private messages.
*   Hiding content that is labeled with themes involving gambling, drugs, sexual themes, or excessive profanity.

For creators, this update carries a practical consequence for reach. If your brand or stream relies heavily on mature-rated games or frequent swearing, you may see a potential drop in concurrent viewers, as a portion of the younger demographic may now be filtered out by these parental settings.
