## New Incentives for Channel Memberships and Platform Enhancements

YouTube has introduced several updates this week that range from direct financial incentives for creators to technical improvements in how videos are presented and distributed globally. From a new bonus program designed to encourage membership adoption to significant changes in thumbnail specifications, these updates aim to provide creators with more tools for monetization and audience growth.

### The YouTube Memberships Bonus Program

One of the most notable updates is the introduction of the Memberships Bonus Program. It is important to clarify from the outset that this is an official initiative offered directly through YouTube Studio; it is not a third-party offer or a scam. 

This program is specifically targeted at creators who are already members of the YouTube Partner Program but have not yet enabled [Channel Memberships](/youtube-tips-en/free-channel-memberships-for-youtube-premium-users-the-big-experiment-in-april-2024/). To encourage creators to activate this feature, Google is offering a one-time activation bonus combined with an additional incentive based on the first 100 members acquired.

The program is structured into two tiers based on the size of the channel:

*   **Smaller Channels:** These creators can receive a €500 bonus for activating Channel Memberships, plus €5 for every new member joined up to the first 100 members (an additional €500), totaling a potential bonus of €1,000.
*   **Larger Channels:** For those with larger audiences, the activation bonus is €1,000, with an additional €10 per membership for the first 100 members (up to another €1,000), bringing the total potential bonus to €2,000.

For creators undecided about enabling memberships, this program provides a low-risk entry point. Beyond the initial bonus, Channel Memberships offer a sustainable revenue stream. When compared to other streaming platforms like Twitch—which often utilizes a 50/50 revenue split—YouTube’s model is more favorable to the creator, who retains 70% of the earnings while the platform takes 30%.

### Improving Shorts Visibility with Frame Selection and Editing

Shorts continue to be a priority for the platform, and new tools are being rolled out to help creators optimize how their short-form content appears in feeds. The "Frame select and edit" feature is currently being deployed, with approximately 20% of creators already having access.

Currently, this functionality is limited to mobile devices, though a desktop version is planned for the near future. This update allows creators to [customize the thumbnail](/youtube-tips-en/coming-soon-to-youtube-custom-thumbs-for-shorts-and-superchat-goals/) of their Shorts directly within YouTube Studio. Rather than relying on an auto-generated frame, creators can now:
*   Select a specific frame from the video to serve as the thumbnail.
*   Add text overlays to provide immediate context.
*   Apply small filters to enhance the visual appeal.

The rollout is expected to take place over roughly three weeks. While there is no fixed date for every user, creators should regularly update their YouTube Studio app to ensure they receive these editing capabilities as soon as they are available for their account.

### Expanding Thumbnail File Size Limits

For long-form content and standard uploads, YouTube is addressing a long-standing technical limitation regarding thumbnail uploads. Previously, thumbnails were capped at a maximum file size of 2MB. This limit has now been increased to 10MB.

This change is a significant quality-of-life improvement for creators who prioritize high-production value in their packaging. As design standards evolve, many creators have moved away from 720p assets in favor of 1080p or higher resolutions to ensure thumbnails look sharp on all screen sizes. Furthermore, the increased limit allows for the use of PNG files—which offer better quality and transparency than JPEGs—without the risk of exceeding the file size cap.

While this is a welcome update, there is still room for growth in supported formats. Requests have been made to the YouTube Studio team to support the WebP format, which offers excellent compression and quality. However, this is still under discussion, and no official confirmation or timeline has been provided yet.

### The Future of Global Reach: AI Dubbing

Finally, there are emerging reports regarding the rollout of [AI Dubbing](/en/video-dubbing/). While a formal announcement is expected later this year during the "Made on YouTube" event, some creators have already reported gaining access to these tools via Twitter.

AI Dubbing is designed to work in tandem with Multilanguage Audio and the Allowed Program. The goal is to allow creators to reach international audiences without the prohibitive cost or time required for manual professional dubbing. By automating the translation and voice-over process, creators can effectively localize their content for different regions.

The potential impact of this feature is particularly high for creators in non-English speaking markets. For example, German-speaking creators could use AI Dubbing to expand their reach into the massive English-speaking market, significantly increasing their potential viewership and ad revenue. 

It remains unclear how extensive the initial rollout will be—whether it will be available to all creators immediately or phased in for a specific percentage (such as 20% or 50%) of the community. For now, creators should keep an eye on official announcements from the "Made on YouTube" event for more details on availability and implementation.
