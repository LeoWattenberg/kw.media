## Recent YouTube Updates: Ad Revenue Bugs and New Collaboration Features

YouTube has recently rolled out several changes affecting everything from monetization and content policies to how creators manage their channels and collaborate with others. For many, the most pressing news involves a temporary technical glitch that impacted earnings for some creators.

### Ad Serving and Reporting Issues

Creators should be aware of a specific window on July 29th and 30th where YouTube experienced significant issues with ad serving and reporting. Between 11:00 AM and 12:00 PM Pacific Time, two distinct bugs occurred simultaneously.

First, there was an issue with ad serving specifically for newly uploaded videos. Because ads were not served during this window, any revenue that would have been generated from new uploads published during those hours is unfortunately lost. 

Second, a separate bug affected [ad reporting](/youtube-tips-en/youtube-updates-mid-roll-ad-bug-and-editing-restrictions-incoming/) across the board for all videos. While this caused confusion in the analytics dashboard, it was a reporting error rather than a serving error. 

It is important to note that while new uploads were impacted, other revenue streams remained stable. [Ad revenue](/youtube-tips-en/youtube-ad-warning-are-you-losing-revenue-dms-return/) for older videos already on the platform, as well as earnings from Shorts and [YouTube Premium earnings](/youtube-tips-en/youtube-premium-bug-why-your-revenue-will-drop-in-march/), were not affected by these bugs. Both issues have since been resolved.

### The Rollout of the Collab Feed

A long-anticipated collaboration feature is now officially live on YouTube, changing how creators can distribute shared content. This feature allows a primary creator to upload a video and then invite other creators to collaborate via a specific link. Once the invitation is accepted, the video appears not only on the original uploader's channel but also on the channels and subscription feeds of all collaborators.

This provides a powerful way to cross-pollinate audiences and increase the reach of a single piece of content. However, there is currently a significant caveat regarding the financial side of these collaborations. It remains unclear whether views and revenue are shared among all participating creators or if they remain solely with the original uploader. Clarification has been requested from YouTube, but as of now, creators should proceed with caution regarding revenue expectations for collaborative posts.

### Enhancements to YouTube Studio and Channel Layout

YouTube is continuing to bring more parity between the desktop version of [YouTube Studio](/en/youtube-tips/) and its mobile counterpart. The playlist tab in the YouTube Studio mobile app has been updated to align with the desktop experience. Creators can now perform more granular management on the go, including:
* Filtering playlists by description and visibility.
* Viewing the total number of views for an entire playlist.

Additionally, there is a change to how Community Posts are displayed on channel pages. If a creator has more than two posts, a "post shelf" will now automatically appear in the bottom row of the channel page. This ensures that community engagement is more visible to visiting viewers. For those who prefer a different look, this layout can be adjusted via the customization tab within YouTube Studio.

### Fan Communities and Engagement Tools

To further foster interaction between creators and their audiences, YouTube has introduced "hearts" for community posts. Similar to how creators can heart comments on their videos, they can now use this feature to acknowledge and highlight specific interactions within their Fan Communities. This adds another layer of social validation and engagement for viewers who interact with Community Posts.

### Updated Profanity Policies for Monetization

For creators concerned about the "ad-friendliness" of their content, there is a significant update to the profanity rules that is now live for all users. 

The updated policy allows for the use of swear words within the first 7 seconds of a video without negatively impacting monetization. This provides more flexibility for creators who use an organic or high-energy intro style. However, this leniency does not extend to metadata. Swearing in titles and thumbnails may still result in limited monetization, as these elements are subject to stricter guidelines to ensure they remain advertiser-friendly across all search and recommendation surfaces.
