## Stricter Rules for Online Gambling Content

YouTube is significantly tightening its policies regarding online gambling content, introducing more rigorous restrictions on how these services are promoted and who can access the content. These changes aim to protect viewers from unregulated gambling platforms and misleading claims.

### Restrictions on Non-Certified Links
The most immediate change concerns the promotion of gambling websites. YouTube now prohibits any reference to URLs for gambling application websites that have not been certified by Google. This ban is comprehensive, covering all forms of communication within a video:
*   **Verbal mentions:** You cannot tell viewers to visit a specific non-certified site.
*   **Visual cues:** On-screen text or overlays showing non-certified URLs are prohibited.
*   **Textual references:** Links in the video description, pinned comments, or other text elements are banned.

### Demonetization and "Guaranteed Wins"
Beyond the certification of the platform itself, YouTube is targeting the nature of the claims made within gambling content. Any content that promises "guaranteed wins" is subject to demonetization. It is important to note that this rule applies regardless of whether the gambling site being discussed is Google-certified or not. If the narrative of the video suggests a sure thing or a guaranteed financial outcome, the video will likely lose its ability to earn ad revenue.

### Age Restrictions and Accessibility
Even if a video does not explicitly violate community guidelines, content that depicts or promotes casinos will now be subject to age restrictions. This means such videos will no longer be available to:
*   Users under the age of 18.
*   Unregistered users who are not signed into a Google account (as their age cannot be verified).

For Creators, this represents a significant hit to potential reach and discoverability, as a large portion of the audience is automatically filtered out.

### Risks and Ambiguities
These stricter rules will likely lead to an increase in [community guideline strikes](/en/youtube-tips/). There remains a grey area regarding specific niches—such as sports betting or "skin gambling" within the gaming community. While these may be viewed differently by some, they often fall under the broader umbrella of gambling. Given the risk of strikes and demonetization, the safest practical step for Creators is to avoid promoting such services entirely until legal clarity is established.

## Expanding E-commerce with YouTube Shopping

YouTube continues to evolve its "Shopping for Creators" features, allowing Creators to integrate their products more deeply into the platform. 

### Setting Up Your Shop
Creators can now manage and set up their shops directly within the YouTube dashboard. This integration is designed to streamline the process of tagging products in videos and Shorts, making it easier for viewers to move from discovery to purchase without leaving the ecosystem.

### Regional Availability
It is important to note that certain features are region-locked. For example, the affiliate program connected to Google Shopping is currently available only for Creators based in the United States. For those operating in other regions, such as Germany, these specific affiliate tools are not yet available. Creators should monitor their dashboard for updates on when these shopping features will expand to additional locales.

## Changes to the Monetization Review Process

For YouTube partners who have met the monetization thresholds—specifically those with over 1,000 subscribers and either 4,000 watch hours or 10 million Shorts views—there is a change in how the [Monetization Check](/youtube-tips-en/youtubes-new-ai-monetization-rules-arent-new-and-more-youtube-updates/) operates.

### The Review Workflow
When a video is uploaded, YouTube Studio automatically runs checks for copyright and ad suitability. Historically, these reviews were completed within a few minutes, particularly for Creators with a consistent history of policy compliance. However, the process involves both machine-based AI and human reviewers to ensure accuracy.

### New Upload Recommendations
Because the review queue can fluctuate in length, YouTube now recommends a longer lead time before publishing content. If you notice your video is fluctuating between the "yellow" (limited or no ads) and "green" (fully monetized) symbols during the check, it is advised to:
*   Upload the video at least **24 hours** before the intended publication date.
*   Ideally, allow up to **72 hours** for the process to complete.

By uploading well in advance, you give YouTube's team and systems enough time to thoroughly review the content, ensuring that your monetization status is stable by the time the video goes public. This prevents the frustration of a video launching with limited revenue only to be cleared later.

### Rollout Status
This change is currently part of a limited test and may not be visible to all Creators immediately. However, it is expected to roll out more broadly in the coming months. Partners should begin adjusting their upload schedules now to accommodate these potential delays in ad suitability reviews.
