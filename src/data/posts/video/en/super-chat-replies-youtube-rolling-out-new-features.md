## New Interaction and Monetization Tools Coming to YouTube

YouTube is rolling out several updates aimed at improving the way Creators interact with their audiences during [live streams](/en/live/) and providing more transparency regarding how content is monetized. From experimental chat features to more precise data on ad suitability, these changes are designed to give Creators more control over their community engagement and revenue streams.

## Enhancing Live Engagement with Super Chat Replies

One of the most notable updates currently in testing is the introduction of [Super Chat replies](/youtube-tips-en/coming-soon-to-youtube-custom-thumbs-for-shorts-and-superchat-goals/), also referred to as "Super Chat threads." This feature aims to transform the linear nature of live chat into a more conversational experience.

### How Super Chat Threads Work
Currently, this feature is in an aggressive experimental phase, with approximately 50% of [Super Chats](/youtube-tips-en/youtubes-end-of-year-updates-jewels-superchat-goals-and-more/) already having the functionality enabled. When a user sends a Super Chat, a small reply button appears. Clicking this button opens a secondary chat window; for those on mobile devices, this manifests as a swipeable panel that can be easily opened and closed without losing track of the main stream.

### The Value of Free Context
A critical aspect of this update is that replies to a Super Chat are free. This removes the financial barrier for further interaction once a paid message has been sent. This functionality provides several practical benefits:
* **Clarification:** A viewer who asks a complex question via Super Chat can provide additional context or clarify their point in the reply thread without paying for another message.
* **Community Interaction:** Other viewers can jump into the thread to answer questions or add their own insights, fostering a deeper level of community discussion within the live environment.
* **Creator Efficiency:** By grouping related messages into threads, it may become easier for Creators to track and respond to specific inquiries during fast-moving broadcasts.

Whether this experiment will lead to a full global rollout remains to be seen, but the current 50% adoption rate suggests YouTube is keen to see how these threads impact viewer retention and engagement.

## Improving Monetization Transparency with AFG Timestamps

For many Creators, the "yellow symbol" in YouTube Studio—indicating limited or no ads due to Advertiser-Friendly Guidelines (AFG) violations—can be a source of frustration due to a lack of specific detail. YouTube is addressing this by introducing timestamped violations.

### Pinpointing Guideline Violations
The new feature provides timestamps that indicate exactly where in a video the AFG guidelines were violated. This increase in transparency allows Creators to identify problematic segments quickly rather than guessing which part of their content triggered the flag. 

It is important to note that these timestamps are not limited solely to the video timeline; they also apply to violations found within the video's title or thumbnail.

### Limitations and Requirements
While this is a significant step forward in transparency, there are specific caveats Creators should keep in mind:
* **Example-Based Reporting:** The system does not list every single instance of a violation. If multiple different violations occur, only one example will be shown. For instance, if a Creator uses profanity in both the title and at several points in the video, YouTube may only highlight one of these instances. Creators will still need to manually audit the rest of their content to ensure full compliance.
* **Self-Certification:** Currently, this feature is only available to Creators who utilize the self-certification process during the upload workflow.

By providing these markers, YouTube enables Creators to make more informed decisions about whether to edit a specific segment or adjust their metadata to regain full monetization.

## Optimizing Reach with "Test and Compare" A/B Testing

Finally, YouTube is expanding its capabilities for thumbnail optimization through a feature now officially called "Test and Compare." This tool allows Creators to perform A/B testing on their thumbnails to determine which visual asset drives the highest [click-through rate (CTR)](/en/youtube-tips/).

### Rollout Schedule
The rollout of "Test and Compare" is scheduled to happen in phases throughout the coming weeks:
* **May 21st:** The rollout begins with a small group (1% of Creators).
* **June 3rd:** Availability increases to 50% of Creators.
* **Throughout June:** The feature will be rolled out to all remaining users.

### Practical Steps for Creators
Because this tool is arriving shortly, now is the ideal time for Creators to begin preparing alternative thumbnails for their upcoming or existing uploads. By having multiple variations ready—testing different colors, expressions, or text overlays—Creators can hit the ground running as soon as "Test and Compare" becomes available in their YouTube Studio dashboard. This data-driven approach takes the guesswork out of packaging and helps maximize the potential reach of every video.
