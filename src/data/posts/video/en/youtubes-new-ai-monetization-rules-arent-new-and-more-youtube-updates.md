## The Truth About YouTube’s AI Monetization Policy

There has been significant discussion recently regarding an "update" to the [YouTube AI monetization policy](/youtube-tips-en/reused-content-monetization-disabled-what-now/). However, it is important for Creators to understand that this isn't actually a new policy. The guidelines governing AI and reused content have remained active and unchanged since 2019.

What is changing is not the rulebook, but the enforcement. YouTube is updating its detection algorithms, which will become significantly more effective at identifying mass-produced content—often referred to as "AI slop"—starting July 15th.

### Creative AI vs. Mass-Produced Content

The core of the policy remains simple: using AI tools does not automatically disqualify you from monetization. You can still use AI in your workflow and participate in the [YouTube Partner Program (YPP)](/en/creator/), provided the resulting content is high-quality and creative.

The risk arises when AI is used to generate low-effort, mass-produced videos. Examples of content that typically trigger these policy violations include:
*   Automated "Reddit stories" with stock footage.
*   [AI-generated fake news](/en/lectures-courses/).
*   Similar types of repetitive, low-value Shorts.

While YouTube may not prevent you from uploading this type of content, it will likely prevent you from monetizing it. If your channel relies on these automation patterns, you may find yourself unable to enter or remain in the YouTube Partner Program. Success on the platform still requires hard work and a dedicated content strategy; there are no shortcuts that bypass the need for genuine quality.

### A Warning Against Automation Scams

With the rise of AI tools, there has been an increase in "YouTube automation masterclasses" and guides. These services often promise access to secret tools or strategies that guarantee entry into the Partner Program for a high fee. 

Creators should be extremely cautious: these are scams. The only people profiting from these programs are the sellers. No tool can guarantee YPP approval because YouTube's systems are designed to reward original creativity, not automated output. If you want to grow your channel sustainably, focus on developing a unique voice and strategy rather than relying on automation shortcuts.

## The Deprecation of the Trending Page

After a decade of service, the [YouTube Trending page](/en/youtube-tips/) is being deprecated. Introduced in 2015, the Trending page was once a primary hub for discovering what the world was watching. However, over the last few years, its visibility has diminished as it was moved deeper into the user interface. Currently, users must navigate through three separate submenus to find it, leading to a natural decline in usage.

### The Shift Toward Exploration Pages

Rather than maintaining a single, general Trending page, YouTube is reallocating its resources toward four specialized exploration pages. This shift suggests a move toward niche-based discovery rather than a "one size fits all" trending list. These new hubs include:

*   **Music:** A chart-like experience showcasing popular music and hits.
*   **Top Podcasts:** A dedicated space highlighting the best of YouTube's podcasting ecosystem.
*   **Movie Trailers:** A centralized location for the latest cinema and streaming previews.
*   **Game Exploration:** A page designed to showcase trending gaming videos.

While a comprehensive game directory might have been a more robust solution than a general "exploration" page, this overhaul is a positive step toward improving how users discover specific types of content.

## Transitioning to Handles in Live Chat

YouTube is also finalizing the transition from channel names to handles within YouTube Live chats. This rollout began approximately two weeks ago and is scheduled for completion by July 14th.

### What This Means for Your Identity

Once this process is complete, you will be identified by your unique handle (e.g., @username) in live chats rather than your display channel name. Because handles are unique identifiers, it is a good idea to secure your preferred handle as soon as possible if you haven't already.

If you decide to change your handle, keep the following technical details in mind:
*   **The 14-Day Lock:** If you change your handle, your previous handle will be blocked for 14 days before it becomes available again (or can be reclaimed).
*   **Privacy Considerations:** For Creators who are concerned about privacy—specifically those who do not want their real name visible in live chat interactions—now is the time to update your settings.

You can manage and change your handle via YouTube Studio by visiting the profile editing section at studio.youtube.com/channel/UC/editing/profile.
