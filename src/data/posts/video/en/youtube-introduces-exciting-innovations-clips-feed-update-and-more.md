## Enhancing Viewer Interaction: New Updates to Clips and Community Features

YouTube is continuing to iterate on how Creators and their audiences interact, focusing heavily on discovery and community engagement. Recent updates and ongoing experiments suggest a shift toward giving viewers more agency in how they highlight content and participate in channel discussions. From an updated Clips feed to the potential for viewer-led posts in the Community tab, these changes aim to deepen the connection between the creator and the audience.

### The Updated Clips Feed: Empowering Viewer Curation

The update for the Clips feed has officially rolled out, providing a more centralized way to manage and discover short highlights of longer videos. A key aspect of this update is its inclusivity: the feed now displays clips created by the Creator as well as those generated by viewers from the channel's content.

This functionality allows viewers to act as curators, picking out the most impactful or entertaining moments of a video and sharing them via the Clips tool. For Creators, this means their content can be rediscovered through the lens of their audience, potentially driving new traffic to full-length videos.

**How to access the Clips feed:**
To find these clips, users can utilize "chips"—small, oval buttons that categorize content—or navigate directly via the scissors icon located on the main page. Additionally, the feed is accessible through the direct URL: [youtube.com/feed/clips](https://www.youtube.com/feed/clips).

### Organizing Subscriptions with Collections

For viewers who follow a large number of channels, managing the subscriptions area can become overwhelming. To address this, YouTube has begun experimenting with "Collections" in the [subscriptions area](/youtube-tips-en/youtube-is-testing-a-new-most-relevant-filter-for-subscription-feeds/).

While specific visual details of the interface are still emerging, the goal is to redesign how subscriptions are organized. This feature comes as a direct response to Creator and viewer requests for better organization tools. While it differs slightly from previous discussions regarding "most relevant" filters, the intent remains the same: improving the user experience by allowing for a more structured view of subscribed content.

### The Expansion of Fan Communities

Perhaps the most significant—and controversial—experiment currently underway is the expansion of the [Community tab](/youtube-tips-en/everything-new-in-youtube-studio-mobile-content-and-community-tab-updates/) into "Fan Communities." 

Currently, the Community tab serves as a broadcast tool for Creators to share polls, images, and text updates. The proposed change would allow viewers to post directly onto a Creator's community tab, effectively turning it into a forum-style space. While this could foster a stronger sense of belonging among fans, it introduces several critical concerns regarding moderation and channel safety.

#### Moderation and Access Control
The primary concern with Fan Communities is the lack of clarity surrounding moderation. It remains unknown how Creators will maintain control over their space. Key unanswered questions include:
* **Posting Permissions:** Will everyone be allowed to post, or will access be restricted to channel members or long-term subscribers?
* **Moderation Tools:** What tools will be available to prevent spam or harassment in a viewer-led feed?

#### The Risk of Community Guideline Strikes
A significant caveat for Creators is the potential for liability. There is currently no confirmation on whether a Creator can be held responsible for content submitted by viewers. If a fan posts "junk" or prohibited content that violates [Community Guideline strikes](/youtube-tips-en/youtube-updates-youtube-studio-app-youtube-app-comments-community-posts/), it is unclear if the strike would apply to the individual poster or the channel owner whose tab hosted the content.

#### The Danger of Centralization
Beyond moderation, there is a strategic risk regarding centralization. By moving community interactions directly onto the YouTube channel, Creators may become overly dependent on a single platform. If a channel were to disappear or access were lost, the entire community infrastructure would vanish with it, as opposed to having a diversified presence across different platforms.

### Looking Ahead: Analytics and Vertical Live

As these features roll out and experiments continue, understanding the data behind them is essential for growth. For those looking to dive deeper into performance metrics, upcoming discussions will focus on [YouTube Analytics](/en/creator/), specifically regarding "Vertical Live." 

Analyzing how vertical live streams perform compared to traditional formats will be crucial as YouTube continues to blend the lines between Shorts, Live, and long-form VOD content. Creators are encouraged to monitor their analytics closely to see how these new interaction tools—like Clips and Community posts—impact overall audience retention and engagement.
