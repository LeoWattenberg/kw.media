## The Timeline for Shorts Monetization

For many creators, the ability to earn revenue from short-form content has been a highly anticipated update. Based on the original publication context of this update, YouTube announced that starting February 1st, 2023, creators would be able to [monetize YouTube Shorts](/youtube-tips-en/monetizing-old-shorts/). This shift marks a significant change in how the platform handles revenue for vertical, short-form videos compared to traditional long-form content.

## Who Qualifies for Revenue?

It is important to note that this monetization feature is not automatically available to every user on the platform. To take advantage of these earnings, creators must be members of the [YouTube Partner Program](/youtube-tips-en/how-to-get-into-the-partner-program-with-youtube-shorts/). 

Being part of the [YouTube Partner Program](/en/creator/) (YPP) is the primary prerequisite for accessing the tools necessary to turn views into revenue. If you are not yet a member, focusing on meeting the YPP eligibility requirements is the first practical step toward earning from your Shorts.

## Understanding the Revenue Model

The monetization of these videos operates under a specific framework described as the YouTube Short A/B testing share model. While traditional long-form videos typically rely on direct ad placements within a single video, the nature of the Shorts feed—where users swipe rapidly between different creators—requires a different approach to revenue distribution.

This share model is designed to distribute earnings across the ecosystem of short-form content. For creators, this means that the financial return is tied to the performance and distribution of their content within this specific sharing framework.

## What to Expect Regarding Payouts

One of the most critical details for creators to understand is how often these earnings are generated. According to the provided information, it is likely that revenue will not be attributed to every single individual video uploaded. Instead, earnings are expected to come from every five to ten Shorts.

This suggests a cumulative approach to monetization rather than a per-video payout. Creators should keep this in mind when planning their content strategy; consistency and volume are key, as the revenue model appears to reward those who maintain a steady stream of uploads rather than relying on a single "viral" Short for their primary income.

By ensuring you are integrated into the [YouTube Partner Program](/youtube-tips-en/everything-important-about-the-new-youtube-fan-funding-partner-level/) and maintaining a consistent upload schedule, you can position your channel to benefit from this monetization structure.
