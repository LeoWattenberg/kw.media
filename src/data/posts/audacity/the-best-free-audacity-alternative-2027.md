Looking for a free Audacity alternative for 2027? **Soundscaper is our pick** if you want an Audacity-like audio editor in your browser, or need a free desktop editor with more mixing and recording tools. It is open source, works without an account for local editing, and can exchange projects with Audacity.

Audacity is still a capable free editor in its own right. The best choice depends on the features you need, so the comparison below includes the trade-offs. It is based on the [Soundscaper comparison](https://soundscaper.org/docs/start/how-soundscaper-compares/) reviewed on November 4, 2026, and Audacity 4.0.2; features may change during 2027.

## Why choose Soundscaper instead of Audacity?

### Edit audio in a browser

Soundscaper Web brings an Audacity-derived editing workflow to a browser. You can record, cut and arrange clips, apply effects, and export audio without installing a desktop editor. Local editing does not require an account. Processing happens locally; optional sharing sends the audio or project archive you choose to the relevant service.

Try [Soundscaper in your browser](https://soundscaper.org/) or use the [audio editor on kw.media](/en/tools/audio-editor/). If you work on a phone, see our guides to [Audacity alternatives on Android](/audacity/want-to-use-audacity-on-android-here-is-how-with-soundscaper/) and [iPhone](/audacity/want-to-use-audacity-on-your-iphone-ios-here-is-how/).

### Get more recording and mixing options

Soundscaper supports timed and sound-activated recording, loop recording into takes, and take comping. Its mixer adds buses, sends, and automation lanes for gain, pan, and effect parameters. Both editions offer macros for repeatable processing, as well as spectral selection and a spectral brush for detailed cleanup.

Audacity 4.0.2 also has real-time effects, clip editing, and project recovery. Its update restored recording autosave and some editing shortcuts, while the audited version's timed recording, sound activation, and macro controls remain disabled. For the details, see the [Audacity release notes](https://github.com/audacity/audacity/releases/tag/Audacity-4.0.2) and our [macro guide](/audacity/missing-macros-in-audacity-4-0-here-is-the-solution/).

### Use plug-ins and optional local models on desktop

Soundscaper Desktop hosts VST2, VST3, and CLAP plug-ins, plus AU on macOS and LV2 and LADSPA on Linux. Native plug-ins do not run in Soundscaper Web. Desktop also offers local transcription, speech enhancement, and source separation after installing optional models and matching engines; availability depends on your machine.

## Can Soundscaper open Audacity projects?

Yes. Soundscaper imports AUP, AUP3, and AUP4 projects and exports AUP3 and AUP4. Check the compatibility report when exporting: project interchange does not guarantee that every feature transfers perfectly. Keep the original project and compare the result before relying on a round trip.

Soundscaper's own format is `.sscape`. Desktop projects reference original imported media by default, so consolidate the media before moving a project without its source files. See the [project file guide](https://soundscaper.org/docs/projects-and-data/project-files/) for details.

## When should you stick with Audacity?

If Audacity already does what you need, there is no subscription to escape and no reason to switch solely on price. It has a familiar desktop workflow, built-in effects, and native third-party plug-in support. Its optional audio.com integration also supports saving and sharing projects, while Soundscaper's optional archive storage is manual rather than automatic sync.

Choose Soundscaper when browser access, extra mixing tools, macros, or its desktop plug-in and model options matter to your workflow. For a closer look at the differences, read the [full comparison](https://soundscaper.org/docs/start/how-soundscaper-compares/).
